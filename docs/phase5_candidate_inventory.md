### Record joins and operational fields

- `shipments.json`: join by `shipment_id`; `route_plan_id` points to the plan; numeric `release_time`/`release_day` and `delivery_deadline` are day offsets; cargo fields include `cargo_class` and `cargo_teu`.
- `route_plans.json`: join by `route_plan_id` or `shipment_id`; ordered legs are one-based in evidence labels below (`L1`, `L2`, ...). Each leg contains `service_id`, directed origin/destination port IDs, numeric `planned_departure` and `planned_arrival`, `min_transfer_hours`, and modeled leg cost. Phase 4 leg IDs use `<route_plan_id>:L<n>`.
- `services.json`: join by `service_id`; includes directed endpoints, `departure_frequency_days`, `transit_days`, `min_transfer_hours`, modeled capacity, cargo classes, and modeled cost. The frequency is used for Phase 4's synthetic periodic departures; the Phase 2 plan leg times remain the fixed planned times used by Phase 3 impact analysis.
- `ports.json`: 6,779 catalog records, including reference rows. There is no separate `operational` boolean in the inspected rows. The 33 ports used by this benchmark network are derived from service endpoints and planned-leg endpoints. Choose targets among those used vertices for operationally meaningful cases; Phase 3 validation itself accepts any ID present in the port catalog.
- Schedule offsets are interpreted from `2025-01-01T00:00:00Z`, with one day equal to 24 hours. Observed planned departures span day 0 to day 399; arrivals span day 2.2701 to day 405.8721. The converted dates are synthetic schedule timestamps, not real sailing dates.

Phase 3 uses half-open event windows `[start_time, end_time)`. Closures check selected operation instants; transfer occupancy is from inbound arrival to the next planned departure and overlaps a closure only when the intervals intersect. Service suspension/delay checks the planned departure instant. A delay shifts the affected arrival while downstream departures remain fixed; Phase 3 checks the resulting connection against the next leg's recorded minimum-transfer requirement. The Phase 4 policy bounds recovery to four replacement hops, a 180-day horizon, three candidate departures per service, and search budgets; modeled individual capacity and cargo compatibility do not represent shared booking availability. `SEARCH_LIMIT_REACHED` is not evidence of route absence. Arrival closures and transshipment handling without supported policy lead to operator-intervention boundaries, not invented vessel movement or handling actions.

The README still calls Phase 3 and Phase 4 future work, while their implementation and reports are present. The Phase 3 JSON schema also requires fields that the validator defaults (`affected_operations` for a closure and `semantics` for a service event). Candidate construction should use explicit fields and the implementation's actual semantics. All candidate times and paths below are observations or hypothetical conditions; they are not final scenarios, notices, impact labels, or verified recovery outcomes.

### Evidence notation and readiness

Candidate IDs such as `C-001-A` are inventory-only identifiers. Evidence references use canonical filenames and keys: `H[shipment_id]`, `R[route_plan_id].ordered_legs[L<n>]`, `S[service_id]`, and `P[port_id]`. `R` refers to `route_plans.json`, `H` to `shipments.json`, `S` to `services.json`, and `P` to `ports.json`. Candidate readiness means only that records/timing were located (`CANDIDATE_FOUND`), further conditions remain (`NEEDS_FURTHER_CHECK`), or none was found. It does not change any Phase 5.1 scenario's `UNVERIFIED` status.

### Candidate-specific cautions

- **SC-001 / SC-004 topology:** alternate directed paths were found by reading the graph; no route schedule, transfer timing, cost, deadline, or validator checks were performed. Do not describe them as feasible recovery plans.
- **SC-005 graph disconnection:** the edge-removal check only indicates no other directed path in the raw network for the listed target edge. Phase 4 may wait for recurring modeled departures if the event window ends; service suspensions, bounded departures, 180-day horizon, deadlines, and search budgets all require a separate later analysis. Do not use this check to assert `NO_FEASIBLE_ROUTE`.
- **SC-007 / SC-008 transfer arithmetic:** Phase 3 compares the delayed inbound arrival plus the next leg's `min_transfer_hours` against that fixed next departure. The computed 2.28 h buffer is a schedule relationship, not a final recovery status.
- **SC-010 shared operation:** eight plans share the same scheduled service departure; an event can therefore produce a multi-shipment cohort with overlapping causes. Reconcile each shipment and preserve one impact record per shipment.
- **SC-011 compound timing:** the suggested delay/arrival window is hypothetical. Closure is checked at the dynamically delayed arrival time; event ordering and any simultaneous downstream connection effects still need exact analysis.
- **SC-012 / SC-013:** these are the strongest no-impact leads, but future scenario construction must recheck every relevant operation in the final window and retain explicit half-open boundaries.
- **SC-015:** no Phase 3 or Phase 4 result was generated. Evidence review is a future agent workflow requirement; the raw schedule records alone do not include operational handling policy.

## C. Coverage and feasibility assessment

- **Promising from record/timing coverage:** all 15 scenarios have at least one plausible data anchor or input-level candidate. The clearest candidate classes are fully specified arrival/transfer closures (SC-002/003), service-delay slack comparisons (SC-007/008), shared same-time compound causes (SC-010), dynamic arrival timing under a compound delay/closure (SC-011), and a service-departure boundary (SC-013).
- **Alternative route coverage:** topological alternatives exist for the selected SC-001 and SC-004 examples, but one includes a three-hop detour and tight initial deadline margin. Independent schedule/cost/deadline checks may require changing candidates. No candidate is yet a verified feasible recovery.
- **Difficult case:** SC-005 is the least ready. Raw edge-removal disconnection and low initial deadline margin suggest bottlenecks, but a temporary suspension can be waited out under Phase 4's periodic schedule model. Scenario timing must be selected and the bounded search behavior understood before deciding whether the case demonstrates infeasibility, deadline limitation, or only search uncertainty.
- **Notice-level case:** SC-014 requires no missing dataset field; its ambiguity is intentionally introduced in a later natural-language notice. Do not assign shipment impacts until clarification produces a valid structured event.
- **Evidence-dependent case:** SC-015 has suitable transfer intervals, but its distinct capability depends on a prior impact result or additional evidence being available to the agent. That result is not generated in this reconnaissance.
- **No-impact coverage:** the frozen records contain clear opportunities at ARBUE/AEQWE in the first simulated day and service departure boundary candidates. Final exact windows still need a separate independent full-scope check.

The principal network limitations remain the synthetic epoch, modeled schedules and service attributes, no shared residual-capacity allocation, no vessel-position state, and no supported arrival diversion or transfer-handling policy. The current recon did not alter source data or run Phase 3/Phase 4 to create outcomes.
